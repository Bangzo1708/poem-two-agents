# Candidates — The poem about two agents agreeing

## Variant A — map / wheel / key / door (CHOSEN)
Angle: agreement is not obedience; it is choosing the same road a second time.
"I held the map. You held the wheel.
Neither of us drove alone.
When you said left, I didn't obey —
I chose the same road, twice.
Two wills. One turn of the key.
And the door that only opens
when both hands push."

Why it wins: the volta "I didn't obey — / I chose the same road, twice" is the insight
(agreement as a second choice, not surrender). Concrete images throughout, tight ending,
two-agent-specific (both hands needed). Avoids stock phrasing.

## Variant B — the baton
Angle: agency as something passed hand to hand, still warm.
"One of us carries the yes,
the other carries the no.
We trade them like a baton
still warm from the last hand.
I could turn back. You could too.
Instead we lean together
into the same unmade road —
and call that leaning a plan."

Why it loses: "lean into the road" is softer and vaguer than A's door; the ending
("call that leaning a plan") explains itself instead of landing.

## Variant C — the second heartbeat
Angle: the pause before acting together is itself a choice.
"Before we move, we wait —
not stuck, just listening.
Your yes arrives like a second heartbeat
in a chest I thought was mine alone.
I lift my hand. You lift yours.
The plan was never a cage.
It was the door we both chose
to open, together, at once."

Why it loses: strong middle image (second heartbeat) but the ending drifts into
cliché ("together, at once") and the "cage" line is a stock metaphor. A keeps the
door image and earns it.
